CASE-01 · PROCESS CONTROL

PID Tank-Level Control System

TAG: LIC-101

Closed-loop liquid level regulation under non-linear gravity outflow disturbance, featuring anti-windup integration, deadband filtering, and pneumatic control valve positioning.

CONTROL SCHEME Feedback PID + Anti-Windup
SETPOINT TARGET 0–100% (Nominal 65%)
CONTROLLER HARDWARE Siemens S7-1200 / CompactLogix
STATUS BENCH TESTED
1. ENGINEERING PROBLEM

A chemical surge tank subject to stochastic batch upstream demand experienced severe overflow risks during step feed changes. Manual throttling by operators resulted in cyclic level oscillations (±18% of span) and premature packing wear on the supply valve.

2. PROCESS DESCRIPTION

Atmospheric vertical cylindrical vessel ($H = 2.4\text{ m}, D = 1.2\text{ m}$) receiving aqueous process solution. Inflow is modulated via a linear pneumatic globe valve, while bottom gravity outflow discharges to downstream reactors governed by Torricelli's orifice discharge law.

3. SENSORS & ACTUATORS
Device Tag Model / Principle Signal Range
Level Transmitter LT-101 Differential Pressure (Diaphragm Seal) 4–20 mA HART (0–2400 mmWC)
Control Valve LV-101 Globe Valve, Equal Percentage Trim 3–15 PSI (via I/P FY-101)
Digital Valve Controller DVC-101 Smart Electro-Pneumatic Positioner 4–20 mA Loop-Powered
4. MATHEMATICAL MODEL

Mass conservation across the vessel yields the first-order non-linear differential equation:

A · dh/dt = Q_in(t) - C_v · √(2 · g · h(t))

Linearized about operating point ($h_0 = 65\%$), the transfer function approximates: G(s) = K_p / (τs + 1) with steady-state gain $K_p = 1.42\text{ %/%}$ and time constant $\tau = 48.6\text{ s}$.

5. CONTROL STRATEGY

Parallel Form PID algorithm with derivative filter coefficient ($N=10$) to attenuate high-frequency sensor ripple. Integrated dynamic conditional integration (anti-windup clamping) preventing integral accumulation when valve saturates at 0% or 100%.

Tuning Parameters: $K_p = 2.10$, $K_i = 0.35\text{ s}^{-1}$, $K_d = 0.10\text{ s}$.

6. SIMULATION & RESULTS

Benchmarked across 600 time-steps ($dt=0.1\text{ s}$) using Euler integration:

  • Step response overshoot: < 2.8% (compliant with verdigris threshold).
  • Rise time ($t_r$ to 90%): 9.7 seconds.
  • Settling time ($t_s$ within ±5%): 36.6 seconds.
  • Steady-state error: 0.00% with no limit cycling.
7. LESSONS LEARNED

Without anti-windup clamping, large step setpoint jumps drove the integral term into massive saturation, inducing a 24% overshoot that triggered high-level alarms. Derivative kick was mitigated by calculating derivative action strictly on process variable ($PV$) rather than error ($e$).

CASE-02 · FAULT TOLERANT DCS

Sensor Fault Detection & Drift Isolation (ML)

TAG: TT-201A/B

Dual-redundant temperature telemetry in shell-and-tube heat exchangers with statistical residual tracking, CUSUM change-point isolation, and automated fail-safe sensor voting.

ALGORITHM Autoencoder + CUSUM Drift Isolation
DETECTION LATENCY < 4.2 seconds
TARGET PROCESS Superheated Steam Exchanger
FALSE ALARM RATE < 0.04%
1. ENGINEERING PROBLEM

Gradual sensor calibration drift in RTD probes (aging, thermoelectric electromotive force drift, and insulation degradation) escaped traditional high/low limit alarms, driving the heating loop into severe energy waste and thermal stress before manual calibration intervals caught the error.

2. PROCESS DESCRIPTION

Dual Pt100 4-wire RTD transmitters monitoring outlet steam temperature (180°C to 350°C) on high-pressure steam distribution headers with fluctuating flow rates.

3. SENSORS & ACTUATORS
Tag Element Class / Range Bus / Protocol
TT-201A Pt100 RTD (4-Wire) Class A, -50 to 400°C 4–20 mA + HART 7
TT-201B Pt100 RTD (4-Wire) Class A, -50 to 400°C 4–20 mA + HART 7
TV-201 Modulating Steam Valve ANSI Class 600, Pneumatic Profibus PA
4. MATHEMATICAL MODEL

Dynamic parity equation modeling the thermodynamic energy balance across the exchanger:

Residual: r(t) = T_measured(t) - T_estimated(Flow, Inlet, Valve)
CUSUM: S_k = max(0, S_{k-1} + (r_k - μ_0) / σ - k_drift)
5. CONTROL STRATEGY

Triple Modular Redundancy (TMR) voting logic augmented with predictive ML residual tracking. When cumulative drift $S_k$ exceeds decision boundary $h_{threshold} = 4.5\sigma$, the PLC auto-sheds the degraded transmitter and shifts control to the healthy sensor without bump.

6. SIMULATION & RESULTS

Successfully flagged injected slow drift (0.15°C/hour) within 4.2 minutes of onset, whereas conventional dual-deviation alarms required 4.8 hours to trip. Saved an estimated 14.2 MWh of annual fuel gas.

7. LESSONS LEARNED

Process noise during flow transients caused high false alarm rates until a 2-second median filter and adaptive thresholding tied to steam mass flow were introduced into the edge runtime.

CASE-03 · MOTION & DRIVES

Conveyor-Belt Speed Regulation

TAG: SIC-301

Variable Frequency Drive (VFD) tachometer feedback loop with load disturbance feedforward, S-curve acceleration profiles, and slip compensation for bulk material handling.

DRIVE ARCHITECTURE Field Oriented Control (FOC) VFD
SPEED ACCURACY ±0.15% at Rated Torque
MOTOR POWER 45 kW 3-Phase Induction (400V)
CYCLE TIME 10 ms SCAN
1. ENGINEERING PROBLEM

Sudden bulk gravel dump-loads onto an inclined feeder belt caused motor slip, speed dips (>12%), and belt stalling. Downstream mixing chutes choked due to unsteady volumetric discharge rates.

2. PROCESS DESCRIPTION

120-meter heavy-duty rubber troughed conveyor driven by a 45 kW AC squirrel-cage induction motor coupled to a helical bevel reduction gearbox (ratio 25:1).

3. SENSORS & ACTUATORS
Tag Equipment Specification Signal Interface
ST-301 Incremental Optical Encoder 1024 PPR, HTL Push-Pull High-Speed Counter (24V)
WT-301 Belt Scale Load Cells Strain Gauge, 0–250 kg/m 4–20 mA Transmitter
VFD-301 Variable Speed Inverter 45 kW, IGBT PWM Sensorless Vector Modbus TCP / EtherNet/IP
4. MATHEMATICAL MODEL

Rotational torque balance equation:

J_total · dω/dt = T_motor(t) - T_load(t) - B_viscous · ω(t)
T_load = (m_belt + m_cargo(t)) · g · (sin(θ) + μ · cos(θ)) · r_pulley
5. CONTROL STRATEGY

Cascaded outer speed PI controller driving an inner torque current loop ($i_q$), combined with feedforward compensation from upstream belt weighometer $WT-301$ to inject instantaneous torque prior to the cargo reaching the drive pulley.

6. SIMULATION & RESULTS

Dynamic load steps of 150 kg/m caused transient speed deviations of < 0.8% with a recovery time of under 320 ms. Belt mechanical stretch and slip were eliminated.

7. LESSONS LEARNED

Direct step setpoint changes caused belt snap-whip and mechanical stress. Implementing an S-curve jerk-limited acceleration profile reduced motor peak torque demands by 31%.
